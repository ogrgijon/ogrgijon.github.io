<article class="tradition-story">
  <p class="tradition-story__lead">December is the month of long nights. Rain, wind and cold are common in Asturias; it may snow at higher elevations, while the coast is usually milder and damp. Winter invites us to seek shelter, but the landscape remains alive beneath the dark sky.</p>
  <h2>The month: draw close and meet again</h2>
  <p>Bare trees reveal the pattern of their branches and streams keep flowing. At home, gatherings and shared meals help fill the short afternoons. Winter is not an empty season: it is a time for care, conversation and preparation for a new cycle.</p>
  <h2>The voice of the cave: the Cuélebre</h2>
  <p>The Cuélebre is a creature in Asturian legends, described as a huge winged serpent, like a dragon. In some stories it guards treasures or protects remote places. A dark cave may be just a cave; the story fills it with mystery and makes listeners wonder what lies inside.</p>
  <p>Here we imagine the Cuélebre guarding what we want to preserve. This is a poetic reading, not a demonstrated original meaning of the legend.</p>
  <h2>Dumannios, an ancient name</h2>
  <p>Dumannios appears in the Coligny calendar, a Gaulish inscription found in France. Its meaning and equivalence with December are uncertain. There is no evidence of an ancient Asturian festival dedicated to the Cuélebre in this month.</p>
