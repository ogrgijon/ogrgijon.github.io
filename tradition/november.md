  <p class="tradition-story__lead">November arrives with short afternoons, rain and cold. Damp days are common on the coast; snow may fall in the mountains. The landscape seems to draw inward, and many cultures have made this a time to remember those who are gone.</p>
  <h2>The month: memory at home and in the community</h2>
  <p>Remembrance does not happen on only one date. It can mean visiting a cemetery, keeping a photograph, repeating an anecdote or saying the name of someone dear aloud. Every family and place has its own ways. Stories of apparitions also speak of death, but through fear and imagination.</p>
  <h2>The voice of the night: the Güestia</h2>
  <p>The Güestia is a procession of souls in Asturian oral tradition. In the stories, a group of figures appears at night. Details vary with the storyteller; it is not a verified event or a belief shared by everyone. Imagine someone returning home and seeing lights in the distance: the legend turns uncertainty into a story that is passed on and remembered.</p>
  <h2>Samonios and Samhain: compare without conflating</h2>
  <p>Samonios is a name from the Coligny calendar, a Gaulish inscription found in France. It is often compared with Samhain, a Gaelic festival associated with the change of season. The comparison does not prove they are the same celebration or that the Güestia comes from Samhain. “Celtic” covers distinct peoples and languages; it is not one identical culture everywhere.</p>
