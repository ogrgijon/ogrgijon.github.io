  <p class="tradition-story__lead">October brings autumn to Asturias. Days grow shorter, temperatures fall and the rains return, though mild days are still possible. Some leaves change colour and fall in the forests; damp paths invite a slower walk.</p>
  <h2>The month: stories at dusk</h2>
  <p>As darkness comes earlier, home becomes a place for conversation again. For generations, stories were told aloud: someone began with an encounter on the road and others listened, asked questions or added details. There was no printed version to fix the tale; each voice made it its own.</p>
  <h2>The mischievous voice: the Diañu Burlón</h2>
  <p>The Diañu Burlón is a character in Asturian stories who tricks, frightens or confuses those who meet him. “Diañu” means devil in Asturian, but in these tales he is a popular, mischievous figure, not necessarily the devil of Christian doctrine. A traveller may think the way is familiar, then discover something has led them astray: the fright becomes a story when told back at home.</p>
  <p>Oral tradition lives because it is told and changes. Asking older people which version they remember also preserves the voice, place and occasion on which they learned it.</p>
  <h2>Cantlos, song and caution</h2>
  <p>Cantlos appears in the Coligny calendar, a Gaulish inscription found in France. It has been linked with singing, though the interpretation is uncertain. It does not prove that October was a month of song or that the Diañu Burlón shares a Celtic origin.</p>
