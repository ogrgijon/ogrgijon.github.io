<article class="tradition-story">
  <p class="tradition-story__lead">June arrives and dusk is slow to come. In Asturias, the eve of San Xuan brings many communities together for a special night. It may be mild, but it can also be rainy or cool: summer here does not always arrive beneath clear skies.</p>
  <h2>The night and the solstice</h2>
  <p>The summer solstice is the day with the most hours of daylight in the year and usually falls on 20 or 21 June. San Xuan—Saint John in Spanish—is celebrated on the 24th. The dates are close, but they are not the same: one is an astronomical event, the other a Christian feast celebrated by communities with their own customs.</p>
  <h2>A celebration shaped by each place</h2>
  <p>Some places light bonfires; elsewhere, the night is marked with music, food and neighbourly gatherings. Certain places have customs connected with water, but they should not be presented as if they were practised in the same way throughout Asturias. The best guide is to ask the people organising the celebration and learn its local history.</p>
  <p>Ancient Celtic rites are often mentioned when people speak of the solstice and fire. Such comparisons are possible, but a bonfire or a nearby date alone does not prove that today's festival directly continues an ancient rite. Practices change, combine and take on meaning in each community.</p>
  <h2>Celebrating with care</h2>
  <p>Fire requires caution. Follow municipal rules and emergency guidance; depending on the risk, bonfires may be restricted or prohibited. The Moon does not determine the date of San Xuan: its phase changes every year, while the feast keeps its calendar date.</p>
