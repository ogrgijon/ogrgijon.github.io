<article class="tradition-story">
  <p class="tradition-story__lead">January dawns slowly. Rain, wind and damp weather are common on the Asturian coast; frost may settle in the valleys, and snow falls in the mountains. There are clear days, of course, but winter is measured not only by cold: it also means fewer hours of light and a slower pace of daily life.</p>
  <h2>The month: resting hills, hidden life</h2>
  <p>Many deciduous trees have shed their leaves. Branches, waterways and footprints in the mud are easier to see. Beneath this quiet appearance, life goes on: animals search for food and plants wait for the moment to sprout. At home, winter was a time to care for livestock, mend tools and gather by the hearth.</p>
  <h2>The voice of the forest: the Busgosu</h2>
  <p>“Busgosu” is the name of a being in some Asturian legends, associated with the hills and forest. There is no single portrait: its appearance and behaviour can change with the storyteller. Imagine a path between bare trees; someone hears a noise off the trail and thinks the forest has inhabitants of its own. That is the beginning of a story, not a scientific explanation of the woods.</p>
  <p>Listening to these stories is also a reminder that the hills are not here solely for our use. We enter carefully, respect wildlife and leave the place as we found it.</p>
  <h2>An ancient name, treated with care</h2>
  <p>Riuros appears in the Coligny calendar, a Gaulish inscription found in France. Its meaning and equivalence with January are uncertain. We connect it here with winter as a literary device, not as evidence of an ancient Asturian festival.</p>
